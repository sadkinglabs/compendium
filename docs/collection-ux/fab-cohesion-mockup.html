BA-Z index rail, and grouped section headers

26 discrete touch targets on a phone is not solvable by making them bigger - there is no room. So they are not touch targets. The rail is one continuous scrub track: press anywhere and drag, and the letter under your thumb is read out in a magnified lens placed to the left of the rail, where your thumb is not covering it. Lifting commits the jump. A single tap still works, it is just a scrub of length zero.

Rarity groups sort by the explicit scarcity rank, not alphabetically. The 36px gutter stays reserved so the grid does not reflow when you change grouping.
Problem the owner should know aboutThe rail and the FAB dock compete for the same right edge. The rail has to stop ~104px above the bottom to clear the docked FAB, which costs it about a fifth of its height and squeezes the letter pitch to roughly 14px. That is workable as a scrub track and would be unusable as 26 buttons - which is the real argument for the scrub, beyond ergonomics. If the letter pitch ever drops below ~12px, drop to a coarse rail (A E I M Q U Z with the lens still reading the exact letter) rather than shrinking the type further.

One write action is primary, the rest are one tap away in the existing sheet chassis.

Confirmation and undo

Destructive, post-confirm. Same shape - the confirm sheet already carried the warning, so the toast does not shout twice.

The honest conflict case from §6. Undo restores only rows still equal to the committed-after value; the rest are reported, never overwritten. No Undo button here - there is nothing further to undo.
Opinion - the toast is the wrong container for this undoA four-second toast is fine for "list duplicated". It is not fine for the undo of a transaction that just rewrote 400 rows, and the brief's own timing concern says that write may take a visible moment on device. Recommend: after a bulk write, the action bar does not disappear - it becomes the confirmation bar ("Added 1 to 42 cards · Undo") and persists until the user exits bulk mode or runs another action. Same real estate, no new chrome, and the undo window matches the weight of the operation instead of a stopwatch.
Problem the owner should know aboutSelection is snapshotted, which is correct. But that means a user can select 42 cards, change a filter, and see a grid where only 9 of the selected cards are visible while the bar still says 42. The bar must never lie, so it keeps saying 42 - which means the surface needs one more honest line when it happens: a thin strip under the selection chips reading "33 selected cards are hidden by the current filters". Not designed above because it only appears in that state, but it is required, not optional.

DThe Unspecified pseudo-set

This plate has one job the others do not: look deliberate. A logo-shaped hole reads as a broken tile, and a 0% completion bar reads as failure. So it gets neither. It takes the warm-brown edge that empty sets wear (it is not a gilt set), keeps full-brightness ink (it is not an empty set), carries no bar and no badge, and states its action plainly.

TriageGrouped by card, allocating across printings. The remainder line ("1 left to place") is the only progress device - it turns jade and reads "All copies placed" when the row balances. Nothing auto-fills.
Suggestions must not look like answersExisting owned printings are ranked first and annotated - "You own 6 Beta cards" - but they start at zero and are never preselected. The annotation is deliberately a count of your holdings, not a recommendation phrased as one ("Probably Beta" would be the app asserting something it cannot know). Sets you own nothing from still appear, plainly labelled, in normal weight, because a first card from a new set is exactly the case a ranked-suggestion UI would otherwise hide.
"Leave unspecified" is a real allocation targetPer §7, variant_slug='' is a legitimate durable state. So the allocation list ends with it as a peer row, not as a cancel. That is what makes the surface a sorting tool rather than a nag: you can open it, deliberately assign one copy to nothing, and the card is resolved. Without that row, every visit would end with something unfinished, which is precisely the inbox behaviour the owner ruled out.
Deviation to confirmThe brief says the plate carries the "Sort printings" action. I have made the plate's only tap target open triage directly, rather than opening a generic card grid with a triage button inside it. A single-target plate matches every other plate's interaction, and the alternative - two hit regions inside one small tile - is the kind of chrome that gets rejected. The cost: there is no way to simply browse your unspecified cards as a grid. If browsing matters, the honest fix is a "View as cards" row inside the triage surface, not a second target on the plate.
